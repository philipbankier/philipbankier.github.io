# Option 2 · PB-0001: The Works

> The site is one engineering drawing of Philip's operation, rebuilt from his ledger every night. Turn the main valve and the camera pulls back. The hero was Detail A on a heavy graphite plant, and red ink soaks into the film only along lines that are live today, with ActRun's pump the largest thing on the sheet. Every section after that is a detail of the same drawing. At the bottom the whole A0 sheet folds itself into an A4 packet, and the title block on the outside is the contact card.

**Build estimate:** 45 days · **Critique scores before refinement** (out of 10): juror 6, engineer 5, brand 7

## Signature moment

COMMISSIONING. Pinned for 280vh (280svh on phones). Everything is drawn by one WebGL2 line engine under a camera mat3 uniform, with no SVG camera group. Snapping is done without ScrollTrigger snap. After 150ms of Lenis scroll-idle, lenis.scrollTo moves to the nearest label (0.6s, power2.inOut), and this is suspended while V-01 is dragged or its inertia runs. The labels are closed (0), open (0.12), wide (0.46), ga (0.74) and dive (1.0). Dragging hand-wheel V-01 maps to lenis.scrollTo(y, {immediate: true}) with touch-action: none on the wheel. Every shot reverses on scroll-up. SHOT 1, OPEN (0 to 0.10): V-01 turns 0 to 270 deg and catches at 30-deg detents on a stepped ease. The stem, drawn in section with 45-deg cut hatching, rises 6px. The tag changes by the site's one change language: an eraser rubs out 'Closed.' in three zig-zag passes over 180ms, a few graphite crumbs fall and settle on the film, and the pen letters 'Open.' SHOT 2, CHARGE (0.10 to 0.22): Red enters the service pipe as a 3px bore inside the 0.7mm double-line pipe, led by a meniscus, with the front on power2.in so it reads as pressure building. Behind the front the red wicks sideways into the film. The engine draws a 24px halo quad whose alpha falls off with distance, and the falloff width is modulated by sampling the same fibre texture the film uses, so the bleed feathers irregularly and runs longer along the fibres. It grows over 500ms and stays. The pipe has been visible since the first frame as a faint pencil line running off the bottom of the viewport, so the reveal is set up honestly. SHOT 3, PULL-BACK (0.22 to 0.46, label wide): the camera scales from 1 to 0.16 on power2.inOut. A detail circle plots around the hero with a leader and the letter A. The hero is now Detail A on an A0 general arrangement, and the drawing has mass. Built equipment is already inked: 2.0mm outlines clamped to at least 1.5 device px so they survive zoom-out, and section-hatched vessels whose 2.5mm hatch resolves to an 80% graphite fill below 0.3x, so the wide frame reads as a black plant silhouette. Only the pipe network ahead of the front is blue pencil. Sub-pixel lines fade by alpha instead of shimmering. Composition, from the build-time netlist: P-101 ActRun is the largest object on the sheet, left of centre, with TK-201 PromptCache inside the dashed Kairox AI battery limit. The Mains coil is a dense serpentine block, then the manifold, the plan rack of four rolls, and the title block at bottom right. Each bubble carries its plain word: A The Plumber, B Products, C Shipped, D Code, E Research, F Contact. From this label on, every bubble is a real DOM link placed from the camera matrix, so ActRun is clickable two gestures after the hero. SHOT 4, FLOOD (0.46 to 0.74, label ga): the front splits at every tee using junction fractions computed from the netlist at build. Pencil inks to graphite wherever the front passes. Red continues only into live lines: both products (in service at the nightly check), the Mains, awesome-agent-skills (pushed in the last 30 days) and any launch from the last 30 days. Repos that are built but quiet ink in graphite with no red. They're drawn as built and standing, with no caps. Three ISA instrument bubbles hold pencil dashes until the flow reaches them, then the eraser clears the dashes and the pen letters real values from the inlined pulse.json: FI-01 ledger entries, FI-02 weld days in the last 30 (with yesterday's reading as a 3% eraser ghost beside it) and FI-03 79 public repos. Launch tees take prebaked red stamps in date order, each soaking into the fibres. The front position is a pure function of scroll progress. Scroll velocity only speeds the dash march inside bores that have already flooded: vN = clamp(|lenis.velocity| / 40, 0, 1) with a 300ms decay, and phase += dt x (1 + 2 x vN). At the ga label the flooded sheet is the site map. Hover or focus on a bubble plots a phantom-line boundary and thickens that region by one ISO pen step. A click flies the camera along the pipe to that detail (Lenis scrollTo, 1.2s, power2.inOut) with the front leading. Arrow keys step between labels only while focus is inside the drawing. This exact frame, exported unaltered, is the nightly og-image. SHOT 5, DIVE (0.74 to 1.0): the camera rides the main-line front into bubble B and scales back to 1, and B's rim grows until it becomes the Products sheet border. There's no cut.

## Intro sequence

There's no loader and no counter. The first response already contains the finished text, and the plotter draws around it. FIRST PAINT (0ms): inline critical CSS paints the film (a 2048px non-repeating fibre AVIF over #E8EAE5, with a fixed lamp pool at upper left), the punched pin-register holes, and the bio sentence and the two connector labels (To ActRun, Write to me) in final position in Mona Sans. That HTML text is the LCP, at about 300 to 600ms. The real h1 is in the DOM, visually replaced by its plotted twin. pulse.json and the weld bitstring (about 2KB) are inlined at build, so nothing waits on a fetch. PEN RULE: every stroke has a fixed window, and speed = arc length / window, using arc lengths precomputed at build. No single pen exceeds 6,000px/s, and when a stroke would, pens are added: pens = ceil(L / (6000 x window)). The intro takes the same time on a phone and on a 4K screen. NAMED EASES: pen is linear. Travel is cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1) for pen-up moves. Out is cubic-bezier(0.23,1,0.32,1). Settle is a spring (420/34) with no overshoot. Swing is a spring (120/9). TIMELINE: 0 to 60ms: shaders compile with KHR_parallel_shader_compile, polled in rAF, and each program is warmed with a 1px draw. Four small pen-tip rings appear on the four registration marks. 60 to 560ms: those four pens plot the outer border clockwise and meet at the corners, with the inner border 100ms behind. 480 to 700ms: zone letters A to F tick down the right edge on steps(1). 520 to 900ms: title block rules plot cell by cell, and the pen letters 'PB-0001', 'Sheet 1 of 5', the date and 'Rev {ledger revision}' once. If fonts or the portrait texture are late, the pen drafts a blue construction grid around the portrait view, capped at 1600ms. 600 to 1900ms: four parallel pens plot the portrait's silhouette, the five index contours outward from the nose summit, the feature lines at pen 0.35 and then the zig-zag break line at the forearm crop. 700 to 2100ms: a fifth pen draws blue cap, waist and baseline guides for two lines, then plots 'Philip' over 'Bankier' as double-line outline letters in blue pencil. 1900 to 2300ms: the WebGL handoff. The 19 intermediate contours ink in as a reveal radius growing from the nose summit, and lamp-weighted widths switch on. 2100 to 2600ms: the name is hatched in. A 2.0mm pen sweeps 45-deg hatch through each letter left to right, 30ms apart per glyph, and the outlines turn from blue to graphite. The settled name is solid graphite mass. The guides are erased to a 3% ghost, and the eraser drops crumbs that settle on the film. 2600 to 3000ms: the identity stamp is pressed onto the portrait's upper right corner, reading 'AI Agent Plumber' and 'Rev {ledger revision}, {last ship date}'. It's a press, not a slam: 90ms from scale 1.04 to 1 with a contact shadow and no screen shake. Three prebaked frames (contact, spread, settled) then crossfade over 350ms while the red soaks into the fibres. Rotation is seeded from the revision. 2900 to 3400ms: V-01 plots at the base of the portrait view, its tag drops on the swing spring, and the pen letters 'V-01 Main. Closed.' and a pencil note, 'Turn to open the main.' 3400ms: the pens lift and park, and idle begins. INTERRUPT: any wheel, key, touch or click before 3400ms ramps the master timeline's timeScale to 6 over 200ms, so it finishes in about 300ms without a jump cut. RETURN VISITS: a same-session revisit (sessionStorage flag in try/catch) shows the settled frame after a 200ms crossfade. A revisit on a later day plays at 1.5x, then draws red revision clouds around everything shipped since the last visit. 'Replay plot' in the title block runs the whole sequence again.

## Portrait treatment

CONTOURED, WITH A LIGHT TABLE. The photo becomes a contour drawing of the man, and the original only appears glowing up through the film when the light table is on, which turns the 449px softness into diffusion. OFFLINE PREP (about 1.5 days, once): rembg removes the park and gives a matte. Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0, unlike Base and Large) gives the depth, which is blurred 2px and then histogram-equalized inside the matte so the face gets about 10 of the 24 levels instead of the arms and shirt. Inside a face-ellipse mask, the contour field is 0.5 x equalized depth + 0.5 x blurred luma, with extra isoline density from a saliency mask on the eyes and mouth. XDoG feature lines from luma (sigma 0.8, k 1.6, tuned threshold) add eyes, brows, lips and hairline at pen 0.35, gated by the same ellipse. The crop is at mid-forearm. PACKING: a lossless RGB WebP with luma in R, depth high byte in G and depth low byte in B, plus the matte as a separate 1-bit PNG. Decode with createImageBitmap(..., {colorSpaceConversion: 'none', premultiplyAlpha: 'none'}), recombine on the CPU and upload as R16F. Sobel runs in the shader. d3-contour runs at build on the exact same recombined field and emits portrait-contours.svg (silhouette, index contours and feature lines, about 18KB gz inline). That one file is the first-frame plate, the no-WebGL and reduced-motion fallback, the title block thumbnail and the plot file. LIKENESS GATE, before any live layer: render the static plate at 200px wide and at full size. Five people who know Philip must name him unprompted. If they can't, tune the luma weight and XDoG threshold until they can, and build nothing else until it passes. LIVE LAYER (the portrait quad in the shared WebGL2 context, 2MP pixel budget): isolines from the field with fwidth anti-aliasing. Every fifth is an index contour at double weight with a small Osifont level number. Line width blends Tanaka's illuminated-contour rule with tone: mix(tanaka(dot(n, L)), 1 - luma, 0.5), running from 1.6px on the side facing away from the lamp to 0.6px on the lit side. The lamp L follows the cursor's bearing on a 900ms spring. Hatching is 45 deg at 2.5mm pitch where dot(n, L) is under 0.45, with a 135-deg crosshatch under 0.25, quantized to 8fps so it redraws like hand re-shading. All patterns are anchored to image space, and line pitch stays at 4 to 6 CSS px, so the source never shows its softness. LIGHT TABLE: hold for 300ms, or use the caption button. Two 40ms flickers, then the film backlights to #FBFBF6 and the photo shows through at about 55%, blurred 3px with its transmission modulated by the fibre texture, with the contours on top as a tracing. On release it fades over 200ms. The same light table works on the traced product screens in Detail B. SVG FURNITURE: a 0.7mm silhouette, a dash-dot centre line, a zig-zag break line at the forearm crop, dimension lines reading 'Source 449 x 561 px' that state the low resolution as a spec, and the stamp. STATES: on load the contours plot outward from the nose summit. At idle the lamp re-shades. While held, the light table. Below 0.5x during the pull-back, the engine swaps in a cached contour bitmap. Caption: 'Contoured from one photograph using estimated depth and tone. 24 levels.'

## The ledger moment

THE MAINS, a coil that works as a calendar and tells hand work from automation. DATA: deploy.yml currently triggers only on push to main and workflow_dispatch. Add schedule: cron '17 6 * * *' (off the hour, because GitHub delays top-of-hour runs) and a data step. scripts/build-data.mjs uses GITHUB_TOKEN to write ledger.json ({date, repo, kind, author class, message, sha, url}), repos.json (README first line, language, last push), pulse.json (ledger revision, weld days in the last 30, previous reading, last ship time, newest ledger SHA), products.json (HTTP 200 checks of actrun.ai and promptcache.live with dates) and newsletter.json (latest issue title and date from livingedge.substack.com RSS, with the line dropped if the feed fails). It fetches the live https://philipbankier.com/data/pulse.json first to carry yesterday's reading forward. The tracker cron's commits are excluded. Commits are classed as automation or by hand from a checked-in list of automation author identities. Every number in copy comes from these files, so nothing is hardcoded. The browser makes no API calls. REV: the ledger revision number plus the SHA of the newest ledger commit in Philip's own repos, never GITHUB_SHA and never this repo's HEAD, which the tracker moves daily. The colophon shows the site-source SHA from git log -1 -- client/ scripts/ package.json. ROUTING: time runs along the +x iso axis at a fixed length per day. At each month boundary the pipe elbows down one level and returns along -x, so the rows of the serpentine are months. It's deterministic, so it stays stable between visits and only ever grows. FITTINGS: solid fillet beads for days Philip committed by hand, small hollow tack welds for days only automation committed, and bare pipe for honest gaps. The launches are the big red fittings: PromptCache on 2026-08-22 as a large tee to TK-201 stamped 'Commissioned 2026-08-22', slop-engine and awesome-image-prompts as tees to labelled outlet flanges, the 2026-08-19 redesign as a union, and tastekit gate valves only for real release tags (the 2026-06-29 package scope move is a weld). Dimension chains measure the real gaps. The BOM counts 'Shipped by hand' and 'Automation' separately, and the caption says it plainly: 'Solid welds are days I shipped by hand. Hollow welds are days only my automation committed.' SCROLL: the coil isn't pinned. The page scrolls through it and the pen head travels each month's run as that row crosses the reading line. Pencil is ahead of the front and ink behind it, and the bore is red only for the last 30 days, so the live end glows and older history sits in graphite. THE OPEN END is honest: 'Last weld 14 hours ago' is computed client-side, with a single drip if it's under 24 hours old. After 2 quiet days it reads 'Line idle since 2026-10-03', and the front stops where the data does. PER VISITOR: the last-seen revision is kept in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch). Welds added since then get red revision clouds and a delta triangle with the count, and the title block reports the same number. Every bead opens a spring-hung inspection tag that links to its commit.

## Type system

Three voices, all forms of ISO 3098 lettering or a reading face. Hierarchy comes from pen width and scale, as on a real drawing. (1) PLOTTED LETTERING: Relief SingleLine (OFL) skeletons, converted at build to about 9KB gz of polyline JSON and drawn by the WebGL line engine with round caps. Hershey is dropped. It's used for anything the pen letters live: view titles, bubble letters and words, balloons, dimension values, tag values and changing title block values. Weight follows the ISO 128 pen series (0.25, 0.35, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.4, 2.0mm), so it changes with zero layout shift. THE NAME: the same skeletons are offset at build into heavy double-line outlines (stroke about cap / 7, round terminals) and then hatched solid at a 2.0mm pen. The display mass comes from the lettering system itself, so there's no second display face. It's set on two lines, Philip over Bankier. Cap height is min(9vw, column / 5.6) on desktop and min(15vw, (100vw - 32px) / 5.6) on phones, computed from glyph advances at build. The real h1 and h2 stay in the DOM, visually replaced by their plotted twins, so the page stays crawlable and selectable. (2) ANNOTATION: Osifont (GPL-3 with font exception, documented in the colophon), set in sentence case at the font's own spacing. It's used for tags, title block cells, captions, labels, end caps and SHAs. Uppercase appears only inside equipment codes (P-101, TK-201, V-01, FI-01). Sizes follow the ISO square-root-of-2 series: 2.5, 3.5, 5, 7 and 10mm mapped to 11, 14, 19, 26 and 37px. Relief SingleLine covers annotation if the licence review objects. (3) READING: Mona Sans variable (OFL), latin subset. Body is 17/26 at wght 420, the lede 22/30 at wght 380, and tables use tabular figures. RULES: no letterspaced uppercase, no italic accent words, no numbered eyebrows, no middle-dot metadata, nothing that scrambles and nothing that counts up. View titles sit under their views.

## Sound design

Off by default. The toggle is an SPST switch symbol in the title block whose lever throws on click. That gesture creates the single AudioContext, routed through a master gain of 0.15 into a DynamicsCompressor, and the choice persists in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch). Everything is synthesized in about 3KB with no sample files, and every exponential ramp targets 0.001. SEVEN CUES, each tied to a physical event. (1) Plotter servo, during the intro and Replay plot only: pink noise bandpassed at 2.2kHz (Q 0.8) with gain following pen speed, plus a 4ms tick on each pen-down. (2) V-01 detents: 15ms triangle bursts through a 3kHz bandpass with playbackRate jitter from 0.9 to 1.1. (3) Flow: pink noise through a bandpass at 300 + 900 x vN Hz, playing only while the red front moves in the commissioning and the Mains, and fading over 300ms when it stops. (4) Stamp press: 60ms of noise through a 700Hz lowpass with a 110Hz sine under it, softer than a slam, and each 'Copied' repeat 12% quieter as the pad dries. (5) Eraser: a 90ms band-limited rub per pass. (6) Light table: two 8ms ticks on the flickers, then a 120Hz hum at -36 dB while it's held. (7) Fold: a short filtered paper crease as each hinge locks. RULES: at most one cue per 60ms, nothing on hover, scroll makes sound only where flow is visibly moving, and peaks stay under -18 dBFS. No music bed. The iOS silent switch mutes Web Audio, which is acceptable. It sounds like a quiet drafting room.

## Performance plan

ONE CONTEXT: a single fixed ogl WebGL2 canvas behind the DOM, scissored to the active regions: the line engine, the portrait quad, the curl and the fold. iOS never juggles multiple contexts. FILM OFF THE GPU: the fibre AVIF is a CSS background that scrolls natively, and the lamp pool is a fixed gradient, so the GPU budget goes to crisp lines at native DPR up to 2. RENDER ON DEMAND: rAF runs only while something moves. The portrait redraws at 8fps for its hatch steps and at 60fps only while the lamp spring or the light table is active. At idle the work is zero, because the impeller no longer runs unattended. LINE ENGINE: one draw call per layer, with the path table updated each frame. Screen-space widths with alpha fades make the 0.16x frame cheap, so there's no SVG-to-Path2D LOD swap to pop. Below 0.5x the portrait comes from a cached contour bitmap. Hatch resolves to solid fill below 0.3x. Code stays under 3k segments through build-time simplification. NO LIVE SVG FILTERS: every stamp is prebaked by resvg-js at build in three frames. LOADING: LCP is HTML text (the bio) at about 300 to 600ms. pulse.json and the weld bitstring are inlined. Shaders compile with KHR_parallel_shader_compile during the plotter's homing and warm with a 1px draw, so the 1900ms WebGL handoff never stalls. GA geometry, Code geometry and the curl and fold programs load on approach (IntersectionObserver, rootMargin 100%), and Draggable, Inertia and Flip are code-split. CLS is 0, because every canvas region reserves its box and pen-width changes are stroke-only. Data stays in WebP and JSON because GitHub Pages can't set headers and won't gzip .bin files. RESILIENCE: context loss swaps in the SVG and AVIF plates. iOS Low Power Mode's 30fps cap is tolerated because every animation is time-based. BUDGETS: about 115KB gz JS in total, about 75KB on first load and about 300KB on first load overall. Total blocking time under 150ms, 60fps on an iPhone 12-class device, and the pull-back prototype must hit that before anything else is built.

## Why this escapes generic design

- Warm cream, a serif and one decorative accent: the ground is cool drafting film and there's no serif. Red is a data channel that marks only things checked or pushed recently, and it soaks into the fibres like real ink.
- Mono uppercase letterspaced labels: gone. Annotations are ISO 3098 lettering in sentence case, attached by leaders to the thing they name.
- Numbered eyebrows and hanging section headers: sections are detail bubbles A to F inside the drawing's own geometry, each with a plain word, and view titles sit under their views.
- The cookie-cutter page order: the page is one general arrangement. Products are equipment paired with traced real screens, the ledger is a coil, the repos are one assembly, the reports are rolled drawings and the footer folds the whole sheet into a contact packet.
- Fade-and-slide-up and SplitText line rises: nothing rises. Things are plotted, hatched in, soaked with ink, cut by a section plane, unrolled or folded, all scrubbed and reversible.
- ScrambleText and count-ups: both are gone. Every changing value is rubbed out by an eraser that leaves crumbs on the film and is then re-lettered by the pen.
- The lanyard badge, the cursor loupe, proximity-weight type and the custom crosshair: all cut. The tag swing is replaced by a DIN 824 fold, the loupe by a light table, and the cursor stays native.
- A thin wireframe hero: the name is heavy hatched graphite mass, and the 0.16x general arrangement is a black plant silhouette with red veins that has to pass a 300px thumbnail test.
- A CSS-filter portrait with a caption claiming a process: the portrait is a contour field of estimated depth and tone that has to pass a likeness test, its low resolution is printed as a dimension, and the original only appears diffused through backlit film.
- Green-square farming: the Mains separates solid welds for hand work from hollow welds for automation, makes launches the dominant fittings and leaves the gaps bare.
- Fake liveness: a stale line says 'Line idle since' with its date, products are red only with a dated nightly in-service check, and the ActRun impeller stays still because no real metric drives it.
- A decorative border and grain overlay: the zone letters are the navigation, the fibre texture is what the red ink travels along, and the grid only appears as scaffolding while a view is drafted.
- Metaphor that lives only in labels: every fitting carries its repo's README first line within one clause, and every device does a job.
- Everything animates: only the pens and live data move on their own. Line boil is confined to the portrait hatching, and idle costs nothing.

- Give the reports a format of their own (juror): they keep their written bodies inside the sheet frame with plate and margin index. Re-setting 33 to 107KB documents as drawing notes is content migration that doesn't pay off in v1 or v2.
- Sound is expected (juror): kept, off by default and cut to seven cues tied to physical events (plotter, valve, flow, stamp press, eraser, light-table tube, fold creases), with nothing on hover.

## How the panel scored the three pitches

- **spectacle**: breathtaking 9, coherence 6, buildability 4. The glass box is the best single idea across the three pitches. - **craft**: breathtaking 7, coherence 9, buildability 8. This pitch has the best rules. The pen is the only actor that moves on its own, red is a semantic channel, every number traces to a JSON file and every scrub reverses. It also has the most honest ledger: a time-scaled pipe with one weld per commit day and the real gaps left visible. - **world**: breathtaking 9, coherence 8, buildability 7. This has the strongest spine. ## Critic verdicts (before refinement)

- **juror (6/10):** The strongest concept of the batch, but it wouldn't win Site of the Day as written. It might get nominated and pick up a Developer mention. The core idea holds: the camera pulls back, the hero turns out to be Detail A on a pencil plant, and red floods only the lines that really shipped. That's ownable and would stick with me. Three problems hold it back. (1) The money shot is visually anemic: pale non-photo-blue hairlines on #E8EAE5 at 0.16x with one 3px red line reads as a faded wireframe in a jury thumbnail. (2) The hero name is spindly Hershey single-stroke with no typographic mass. (3) Half the interactions are effects I can name in one second: the Vercel lanyard, the Lando loupe, ScrambleText everywhere, the SplitText line rise, count-ups, the stamp slam with screen shake, and cursor-proximity weight. It's also over-built. About 940vh of pinned scroll across three signature-scale sequences breaks the brief's own one-signature rule. The products, which are the actual business, get the least love. Cut 40%, give the drawing real ink mass, swap the cloned effects for drafting-native physics (ink wicking, eraser crumbs, light table, DIN fold) and it becomes a contender.
- **engineer (5/10):** Buildable on static GitHub Pages: nothing in it needs a server. As written, though, several core recipes are wrong in ways that would show up on first load.

1. The intro timing doesn't add up. Pen speeds can't fit the stated windows: the portrait comes out 12 to 20x over, the sheet border about 5x over, and the name about 1.5 to 2x over at 1440px. Because speeds are in px/s, the intro also runs longer on bigger screens.
2. The LCP claim is false. Inline SVG paths aren't LCP candidates, and the h1 and bio are hidden, so LCP lands around 2.9s or later.
3. The camera can't be an SVG group. Scaling 1,500 paths repaints every frame, line weights disappear at 0.16x, and DrawSVG reveals break under non-scaling strokes.
4. The portrait data packing bands again. WebP stores 8 bits per channel, and data kept in alpha gets premultiplied.
5. "Flow speed = 1 + |v| x 6" contradicts a reversible scrub and has no units.

Data reality: as of 2026-10-07, only awesome-agent-skills (plus the site repo) pushed in the last 30 days. So the FLOOD shows 7 of 8 branches capped and Detail D shows 1 red bore. Stars are 20, not the 15 in the copy, and three of the eight repos have 0. Products have no public commit signal to gate on.

Repo reality: the current site is a React 19 + wouter SPA rendering into an empty #root, and `build:gh` copies index.html to 404.html. The 'existing Vite multi-page toolchain' doesn't exist yet. The reports are raw standalone HTML in client/public/pages (33 to 107KB each), and Vite doesn't process them.

Fix: one WebGL2 plotter engine for all line work, a CSS film layer, stamps prebaked at build, and a netlist-generated GA. With those, the concept holds and the signature moment runs at 60fps on an iPhone 12-class phone. Full scope is about 50 days for one strong engineer. A v1 with the hero, commissioning, Mains, Plant, tag and frame is about 24 days, with D and E deferred.
- **brand (7/10):** This mostly reads as a serious builder. Drafting conventions are used with real rigour, and the honesty mechanics (capped dead branches, 'Line idle since', the low resolution stated as a dimension) build more trust than any adjective could. The copy samples have no em dashes, emoji, exclamation marks or 'not X, it is Y' lines. Where it slips into costume, the data is weak and the metaphor gets amplified to cover it: 300vh of a bot's commits to an awesome-list as the main proof, star counts of 15 and below printed as dimensions, and ActRun's pump spinning at Philip's personal commit rate. The Rev SHA has a live hazard too. On this repo it points at the daily Yohei Nakajima tracker commits, so a founder who clicks the hero stamp lands on an intelligence dossier about another founder. Fix those four and it's an 8.5. As written it's a 7.
